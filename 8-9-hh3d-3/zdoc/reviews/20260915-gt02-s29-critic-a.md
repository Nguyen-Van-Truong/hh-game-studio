## Blocking finding F1 — terminal fsync failure still produces COMMITTED lookup

Priority: P1. Requirements: TX11, TQ02 and the durable receipt/UNKNOWN contract.

At `studio/host/core/journal.py:391`, `_append` writes and flushes the complete
terminal line, then calls `os.fsync` at line 394. An fsync exception raises
`JOURNAL_WRITE_FAILED` at line 396 but leaves the readable terminal line in place.
There is no confirmed durable boundary or failure state checked by subsequent
reads. The transport catches the failure at `studio/host/core/transport.py:689`,
removes the active job and stops admission, as intended. Its comment at line 690
promises UNKNOWN on a durable write failure.

However, `studio/host/core/transport.py:557` reloads the journal and returns the
complete terminal receipt. Only an orphaned ACCEPTED_PENDING receipt is changed
to UNKNOWN. `_existing` at line 566 also returns that terminal receipt for retry.
Thus a client receives COMMITTED/READBACK_CONFIRMED even though the only terminal
fsync failed and recovery has not confirmed durability. Stopping the host does
not prevent this false success response.

Observed with real loopback requests and an independent fault injected only at
the terminal fsync, after durable PENDING, one actual fixture effect and readback:

```json
{"fsync_calls":1,"host_stopped":true,"effects":1,"lookup_status":"COMMITTED","lookup_code":"READBACK_CONFIRMED","retry_status":"COMMITTED","diagnostic":"FIXTURE_RECOVERY_REQUIRED"}
```

A second independent disposable fixture then replaced `host.journal` with a new
`Journal` instance while the fsync fault remained armed. Observed:

```json
{"fresh_journal_lookup":"COMMITTED","fsync_calls":1,"stopped":true,"effects":1}
```

Both diagnostic reproductions exited 0 because their assertions confirmed the
defect. They are not acceptance PASS results. No physical disk failure was
induced; the failing durability primitive is deterministic fault injection.
Reading the complete line back does not establish successful fsync.

Required outcome after a fix: an unresolved failed durability step must not
authorize COMMITTED through lookup, retry or a replacement Journal instance.
The already applied effect must remain at one; do not report no-effect or
silently execute it again. Add regressions at write/flush/fsync failure points,
including failure of recovery itself and re-open through a separate instance.

Remedy assessment requested by coordinator: an object-local or process-memory
poison flag alone is insufficient, as the second probe demonstrates. A durable
uncertainty protocol is one option. It must not depend on successfully writing a
new marker only after storage has already failed. Another possible design is
rollback under the permanent guard to the last confirmed durable offset, with
successful truncation and fsync before clearing uncertainty. After a failed
terminal append this preserves the earlier PENDING, which must resolve to
UNKNOWN if the effect already occurred. If rollback/truncation/fsync also fails,
new instances and reads must remain fail-closed. Explicit recovery that validates
and successfully synchronizes a retained complete terminal record before
authorizing its receipt is another possible approach. This review does not
certify any unimplemented recovery primitive or require a particular marker
format; the externally observable invariant above is mandatory.
